12 Bible verses to text a friend who is struggling

November 2026 · Verses quoted from the King James Version

When someone you love is having a hard week, a long message can feel like too much to write and too much for them to read. A single verse and one honest line from you often says more. Here are twelve short Bible verses that fit in a text, grouped by what your friend might be going through, with a note on when each one helps.

When they are grieving

Psalm 34:18

“The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit.”

Good for the first days after a loss. It doesn't explain anything or ask them to feel better. It just says God is close.

Psalm 147:3

“He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds.”

Short enough to read at a glance, and gentle about healing taking time.

Lamentations 3:22–23

“It is of the LORD’s mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness.”

Written by someone grieving the fall of a whole city. Send it in the morning, when the hardest part of grief is often just getting up.

When they are anxious or afraid

Isaiah 41:10

“Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness.”

For a friend waiting on test results, a court date or a job decision.

Philippians 4:6–7

“Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God. And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.”

“Be careful for nothing” means “don't be anxious about anything.” Worth adding that you'll pray with them, so it reads as an invitation, not an instruction.

1 Peter 5:7

“Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you.”

The shortest verse here, and one of the easiest to remember at 2 a.m.

John 14:27

“Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you. Let not your heart be troubled, neither let it be afraid.”

Jesus said this the night before He died, to friends who were about to be very afraid.

If your friend's worry has gone on for a while, there's a longer list of Bible verses for anxiety, with a way to pray each one that you could pass along.

When they are worn out

Matthew 11:28

“Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.”

For new parents, carers, people working two jobs, anyone running on empty.

Psalm 46:1

“God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.”

“Very present” is the part to notice: not distant, not later, but now.

When they are facing something new

Joshua 1:9

“Have not I commanded thee? Be strong and of a good courage; be not afraid, neither be thou dismayed: for the LORD thy God is with thee whithersoever thou goest.”

A classic for a move, a new job, a first week at university or a diagnosis that changes plans.

Deuteronomy 31:8

“And the LORD, he it is that doth go before thee; he will be with thee, he will not fail thee, neither forsake thee: fear not, neither be dismayed.”

Moses said this to Joshua before handing over leadership. It works well the night before a big day.

Psalm 121:8

“The LORD shall preserve thy going out and thy coming in from this time forth, and even for evermore.”

A good one to send someone who is travelling.

How to send a verse so it lands well

  • Add one line of your own. “Thinking of you today, this one made me think of you” turns a verse into a message from a friend.
  • Don't ask for a reply. People who are struggling often can't answer. Say so: “No need to reply.”
  • Be careful with Romans 8:28. “All things work together for good” is true, but in fresh grief it can sound like you're saying the loss was good. Save it for later.
  • Follow up. A verse on day one and a “still praying for you” a week later means more than five verses in one day.
